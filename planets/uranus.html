Uranus

The tilted ice giant that rolls around the Sun. Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the first discovered with a telescope, by William Herschel in 1781.

Overview

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the first discovered with a telescope, by William Herschel in 1781. Its pale cyan colour comes from methane in its atmosphere, which absorbs red light.

Uranus is tipped over by about 98°, so it essentially rolls around the Sun on its side. This gives it the most extreme seasons in the Solar System, with each pole facing the Sun for about 42 years.

Atmosphere

Hydrogen and helium with a few per cent methane. Uranus has recorded the coldest atmospheric temperatures of any planet, around −224 °C.

Structure, rings and moons

Beneath its atmosphere, Uranus is thought to be made largely of a hot, dense fluid of water, methane and ammonia ices around a small rocky core. It has 13 known faint rings and more than two dozen moons, named after characters from Shakespeare and Pope.

Did you know?

  • The first planet discovered in the telescope era.
  • Rotates on its side, likely after an ancient giant collision.
  • Its moon Miranda has cliffs up to about 20 km high.

How to observe Uranus

At its brightest, Uranus is just at the limit of naked-eye visibility under very dark skies. Binoculars and a star chart will reveal it as a small greenish-blue point.

Uranus FAQ

Why is Uranus tilted?

The leading theory is that one or more huge collisions early in its history knocked the planet onto its side.

What colour is Uranus?

Uranus looks pale blue-green because methane in its atmosphere absorbs red light and reflects blue and green.

Has any spacecraft visited Uranus?

Only Voyager 2, which flew past in January 1986.
