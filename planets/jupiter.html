Jupiter

Overview

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and by far the largest — more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined. Over 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.

This gas giant is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its striped clouds are bands of fierce winds, and its famous Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has raged for centuries.

Atmosphere

Roughly 90% hydrogen and 10% helium, with traces of ammonia, methane and water. Deep inside, enormous pressure turns hydrogen into a metallic liquid that generates a powerful magnetic field.

Structure, rings and moons

Jupiter has no solid surface. Its four large Galilean moons are worlds in their own right: volcanic Io, ice-covered Europa with its hidden ocean, giant Ganymede and heavily cratered Callisto.

Did you know?

  • Has the shortest day of any planet — under 10 hours.
  • Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System, bigger than Mercury.
  • Jupiter has a faint ring system, discovered by Voyager 1 in 1979.

How to observe Jupiter

Jupiter is one of the brightest points in the night sky. Steady binoculars reveal up to four Galilean moons in a line beside it; a small telescope shows its cloud belts and, at times, the Great Red Spot.

Jupiter FAQ

How many moons does Jupiter have?

More than 90 moons have been confirmed, and new small ones are still being discovered.

Could you stand on Jupiter?

No. Jupiter has no solid surface — its gases simply get denser and hotter with depth.

How big is the Great Red Spot?

It is currently about 1.3 times Earth's diameter and has been shrinking slowly over the past century.
