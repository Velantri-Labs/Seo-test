Overview

Neptune is the eighth and most distant planet from the Sun. It was the first planet found through mathematics: Urbain Le Verrier predicted its position from wobbles in Uranus's orbit, and Johann Galle spotted it in 1846.

Though cold and dark, Neptune is extraordinarily dynamic. It has the fastest winds in the Solar System, reaching about 2,000 km/h, and giant dark storms that come and go.

Atmosphere

Hydrogen, helium and methane give Neptune its deep blue colour. Voyager 2 photographed the Great Dark Spot, a storm about the size of Earth, in 1989.

Structure, rings and moons

Like Uranus, Neptune is an ice giant with a thick mantle of water, ammonia and methane ices above a rocky core. Its largest moon, Triton, orbits backwards and has geysers of nitrogen gas.

Did you know?

  • Completed its first orbit since discovery in 2011.
  • Triton is probably a captured Kuiper belt object.
  • Sunlight takes about four hours to reach Neptune.

How to observe Neptune

Neptune is never visible to the naked eye. With binoculars or a small telescope and a good finder chart, it appears as a tiny blue-grey star.

Neptune FAQ

Why is Neptune so windy?

Scientists are still investigating, but low friction and internal heat escaping from the planet likely help drive its supersonic winds.

How was Neptune discovered?

Its position was predicted mathematically by Urbain Le Verrier and confirmed by Johann Galle at the Berlin Observatory in 1846.

Is Neptune the coldest planet?

Neptune is extremely cold, but Uranus has recorded the lowest atmospheric temperatures.
