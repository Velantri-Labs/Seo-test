Mars

The dusty red planet of giant volcanoes. Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun — a cold, dusty desert world with a thin atmosphere.

Overview

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun — a cold, dusty desert world with a thin atmosphere. Iron oxide (rust) in its soil gives it the distinctive red colour that earned it the name of the Roman god of war.

Billions of years ago Mars was warmer and wetter, with rivers, lakes and possibly a shallow ocean. That watery past makes it one of the best places to search for signs of ancient life.

Atmosphere

The thin atmosphere is about 95% carbon dioxide, with surface pressure less than 1% of Earth's. Planet-wide dust storms can hide the surface for weeks.

Surface and geology

Mars hosts Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System at around 22 km high, and Valles Marineris, a canyon system over 4,000 km long. Its poles are capped with water ice and frozen carbon dioxide.

Did you know?

  • Its two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, may be captured asteroids.
  • Sunsets on Mars appear blue because of how fine dust scatters light.
  • Ingenuity made the first powered flight on another planet in 2021.

How to observe Mars

Mars looks like a steady orange-red star. It is brightest and largest around opposition, which happens roughly every 26 months, when Earth passes between Mars and the Sun.

Mars FAQ

Why is Mars red?

Its surface dust and rocks are rich in iron oxide — essentially rust — which reflects red light.

Does Mars have water?

Yes, mostly as ice in its polar caps and underground. Liquid water flowed on the surface billions of years ago.

How long does it take to get to Mars?

With current rockets, a trip takes around seven months, launched during windows that open about every 26 months.
