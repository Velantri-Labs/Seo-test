Milestones

Seven decades of reaching outward

From a beeping metal sphere to a golden-mirrored telescope a million miles away — the moments that changed how we see the sky.

  1. 1957

    Sputnik 1

    The first artificial satellite orbits Earth, opening the Space Age.

  2. 1969

    Apollo 11

    Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin become the first humans to walk on the Moon.

  3. 1977

    Voyager 1 & 2

    Twin probes launch on a grand tour of the outer planets that still continues today.

  4. 1990

    Hubble Space Telescope

    A telescope above the atmosphere begins delivering the sharpest views of the universe yet.

  5. 2012

    Into interstellar space

    Voyager 1 crosses the heliopause, becoming the first human-made object to leave the Sun's bubble.

  6. 2021

    James Webb Space Telescope

    The largest space telescope ever launched begins peering at the earliest galaxies in infrared light.

FAQ

Questions curious minds ask

How many planets are in the Solar System?

There are eight planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet by the International Astronomical Union in 2006.

Which planet is the largest?

Jupiter is the largest planet. It is more than eleven times wider than Earth and more massive than all the other planets combined.

Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?

Venus has a thick carbon dioxide atmosphere that traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, keeping its surface around 465 °C — hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

How long does sunlight take to reach Earth?

Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to travel the roughly 150 million kilometres between the Sun and Earth.

Do I need a telescope to see the planets?

No. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn are all visible to the naked eye at the right time of year. Binoculars reveal Jupiter's four largest moons, and a small telescope shows Saturn's rings.
